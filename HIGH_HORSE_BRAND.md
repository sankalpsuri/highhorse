# High Horse — Brand Document

This is the permanent source of truth for the High Horse website, landing pages, proposals, sales material, outreach, case studies and future brand communication. Any AI, developer, designer or copywriter should be able to work from this document without additional context.

---

## 1. Brand Foundation

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Brand Name | High Horse |
| Business Category | Search Marketing Agency |
| Tagline | We turn intent into revenue. |
| Supporting Brand Line | Understand broadly. Solve narrowly. |

**What High Horse Is**

High Horse is a business-aware Search Marketing Agency. Before building a search strategy, we study a business's:

- Market and industry
- Products and services
- Customers and buying journey
- Competitors and alternatives
- Business economics
- Existing marketing performance

Search remains our core specialisation. High Horse is not a general business consultancy and is not a full-service marketing agency.

**Core Belief**

Customers are already searching for products, services and solutions. The problem is that businesses often fail to:

- Appear when valuable customers search
- Capture the right search demand
- Spend advertising budgets efficiently
- Convert visitors into qualified enquiries or sales
- Follow up with search-generated leads effectively
- Connect marketing activity to actual revenue

High Horse helps businesses find and fix these leaks.

**Brand Principle**

Understand the whole business. Own the search problem. Recommend honestly beyond it.

## 2. Positioning

**Primary Positioning**

High Horse helps established businesses capture more of the demand that already exists for their products or services and convert it into qualified leads, sales and measurable revenue.

**What High Horse Is Not**

- A generic digital marketing agency
- A social media management agency
- An agency that sells disconnected services
- A cheap SEO package provider
- A campaign operator selling hours or deliverable counts
- A website company that happens to offer marketing
- An AI agency following trends without business relevance
- A company promising guaranteed rankings, leads or revenue
- A company claiming that every business should invest in search

---

## 3. Ideal Customer

**Ideal Customer — must have all of the following**

| # | Criterion | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Real search demand | People are already searching for their category, products, services or customer problems. |
| 2 | Established or well-funded | Proven offer, working operations, enough commercial stability to evaluate marketing properly. |
| 3 | Serious marketing budget | Treats marketing as an investment; can afford proper research, execution, advertising and measurement. |
| 4 | Decision-maker access | High Horse should have access to the founder, CEO, CMO, marketing head or person accountable for revenue. |
| 5 | Strong business economics | The value of acquiring additional customers must justify the cost of search marketing. |
| 6 | Can fulfil additional demand | Operational and sales capacity to handle more enquiries or sales. |

Business size alone does not determine fit. Business readiness matters more than whether the business is small or large.

**Poor-Fit Customers — avoid businesses that:**

- Have little meaningful search demand
- Want the cheapest marketing provider
- Have no realistic marketing budget
- Are experimenting with marketing for the first time without patience
- Do not provide access to a decision-maker
- Refuse to share sales or performance information
- Expect guaranteed results
- Have weak fulfilment or sales capability
- Want unrelated services bundled under one small retainer
- Judge performance only through vanity metrics
- Are unwilling to install proper tracking

---

## 4. Customer Problems

| # | Problem | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bad results or ROI | Marketing generates clicks, impressions or leads but not enough qualified business. |
| 2 | Leaked advertising spend | Wasted through irrelevant searches, poor targeting, weak campaign structures, low-value keywords, bad location targeting, weak landing pages, poor conversion paths, missing negative keywords, inadequate tracking. |
| 3 | Poor agency–client communication | Clients don't clearly understand what happened, why, what's changing, what's next, or what they need to do. |
| 4 | No clear ROI measurement | Businesses can't confidently connect search, visits, calls, forms, WhatsApp enquiries, qualified leads, sales and revenue. |
| 5 | Lost search demand | Competitors, marketplaces, aggregators, directories or AI answers capture demand first. |
| 6 | Weak conversion | Traffic arrives but doesn't convert due to weak offers, poor landing pages, missing trust, slow response, confusing websites, broken forms, poor lead handling. |

## 8. How High Horse Works

**Step 1 — Understand the Business**: market, products, customers, competitors, business economics, customer journey, existing marketing and sales process.

**Step 2 — Understand Search Demand**: what customers search, why they search, which searches show buying intent, which locations matter, which competitors capture the demand, which parts of the journey happen through search.

**Step 3 — Find the Leaks**: across paid search, organic search, local search, AI-search visibility, advertising spend, website conversion, lead capture, follow-up, tracking and attribution.

**Step 4 — Prioritise the Opportunity**: rank actions by business impact, supporting evidence, confidence, cost, effort, time to result.

**Step 5 — Execute and Improve**: implement and improve the selected search strategy.

**Step 6 — Measure Commercial Results**: Search → Visit → Enquiry → Qualified Lead → Sale → Revenue.

**Internal Methodology**
Working name: **Search Revenue Engine** — High Horse's internal methodology for understanding demand, identifying leaks, prioritising opportunities, managing experiments and measuring search-generated business results. Do not present it as finished proprietary software. The prospect should understand the business outcome before being introduced to the methodology; it should not be the main website pitch until validated through repeated client work.

**Search Opportunity Brief**
A possible primary entry point — a short brief showing: one meaningful search-demand pattern, one relevant competitor or market threat, one visible leak, the likely business consequence, and why the opportunity is worth investigating. The website must not promise every visitor automatically receives a detailed free audit — the CTA should create a conversation and allow High Horse to qualify the business first.

## 10. Differentiation

1. **Business Context Before Campaigns** — High Horse doesn't start by selling SEO or Google Ads; it first understands market, product, customer, competition and commercial model.
2. **Search Specialisation** — focused on people actively searching for products, services and solutions.
3. **Paid, Organic, Local and AI Search Together** — treated as one customer journey, not disconnected services.
4. **Commercial Measurement** — the objective is qualified business and measurable revenue, not activity reports.
5. **Diagnosis Before Prescription** — identify the real constraint before recommending execution.
6. **Honest Strategic Advice** — if search isn't the best direction, High Horse says so rather than forcing its services.
7. **Direct Accountability** — clients understand who is accountable, what is changing, and why.

**Competitor Positioning**

| Competitor type | Limitation |
|---|---|
| Generic Agencies | Sell many services but often lack specialisation and clear accountability. |
| SEO Agencies | May focus on rankings/traffic without owning conversion or revenue measurement. |
| Paid-Search Agencies | May generate leads quickly but ignore organic visibility, website conversion, long-term demand capture. |
| AI-Search Agencies | May focus on citations/visibility without connecting to business performance. |
| High Horse | Connects business understanding, search demand, paid and organic visibility, conversion and commercial measurement. |

Do not claim no competitor offers similar services, or that High Horse is the only agency that does this.

## 12. Writing Rules

**Use:** short paragraphs, clear headings, specific observations, plain English, business outcomes, evidence-backed claims, direct calls to action.

**Avoid:** corporate filler, long blocks of vague copy, excessive marketing jargon, fake urgency, generic praise, empty superlatives, trend-heavy AI terminology, unnecessary abbreviations, repeating the same point, starting every section with "At High Horse."

**Outcome-Led Copy Rule** — rewrite activity statements as outcome statements:

| Don't write | Write instead |
|---|---|
| "We improve your SEO rankings." | "We help you capture more customers already searching for what you sell." |
| "We manage Google Ads." | "We reduce wasted search spend and direct more budget towards searches with stronger commercial intent." |
| "We build high-converting landing pages." | "We improve the journey from search to qualified enquiry or sale." |
| "We provide detailed reports." | "We show which search activity is creating qualified leads, sales and revenue." |

---

## 14. Claims and Evidence Policy

- Never invent numbers.
- Never invent case studies.
- Never imply guaranteed causation.
- Never claim a business is losing revenue unless reliable data proves it.
- Use "may", "appears", "could" or "worth investigating" for unconfirmed observations.
- Clearly distinguish leads from qualified leads.
- Clearly distinguish pipeline from closed revenue.
- Clearly distinguish correlation from direct attribution.
- Do not claim proprietary software exists unless later confirmed.
- Do not publish customer names or results without approval.
